The brief My first ever project

Learning costs are high, access to resources is unequal and educational opportunities are limited. Learneez is a skill sharing app that replaces money with a credit based exchange: users trade skills directly, or earn credits by teaching and spend them on lessons.

Objectives and goals

Make learning accessible to everyone, whatever their budget. Users grow together by learning from each other and sharing their own skills, so everyone has a way to contribute.

User challenges

Users may feel unsure about how to price their skills with credit points. Scheduling conflicts and doubts about skill quality could discourage participation.

Platform challenges

Keeping users interested after a few swaps, standing out from similar platforms, balancing skills in high and low demand, and growing a diverse user base.

Research

Desk research showed a shift toward hybrid learning that combines digital education with real life interaction. Users want more interactive, community driven experiences, and gamification such as credit points motivates them by making learning rewarding.

I compared platforms that offer parts of the idea, and looked at where each one falls short.

What I took from it

Skillshare

Online courses

An extensive library of well structured online courses. No real life interaction or skill sharing focus.

Adapt the model to in person sessions and local meetups.

Tandem

Language exchange

Strong for language exchange, with real time learning. Focused only on language learning.

Expand to other skills and introduce a credit system.

Information structure

Categories are the primary structure. Features, time and location are the secondary structures.

Primary structure

Categories

Skills are organised into clear, searchable groups such as Art & Design, Languages or Culinary Skills.

Secondary structures

Features

Skill matching and scheduling, with the credit point system and personalised profiles.

Time

An integrated calendar and notifications help users plan and manage their lessons.

Location

Location based filters help users find skill partners nearby for in person exchanges.

The name

Learneez combines "learn" and "ease", reflecting the goal of making learning easy and accessible through simple, community driven skill exchanges. It stands for a straightforward way to gain new skills by trading time and knowledge.

The logo

The green figure represents guidance, growth and knowledge transfer. The purple figure symbolises learning, curiosity and personal development. Together they show the core mission: mentorship and collaborative learning.

The app design

The app follows a learner from signing up to booking a lesson with someone they have found.

Getting started

Create an account, then explore skills from the home screen.

Discovering people

Swipe through suggested people, or browse skills that may interest you.

Saving and searching

Skills and people the user likes are saved in two lists, one for categories and one for profiles. The search screen finds skills and people, and lists what was searched before.

Profiles and reviews

A profile shows requests, credit points, a calendar and skills. Other users’ profiles have information, photos and reviews, with Book a lesson and Direct swap.

Booking a lesson

The user chooses a date, then a time slot, and receives a confirmation.

Reflection

What I learned

Engaging directly with users

The research gave me a deeper understanding of why it matters to engage directly with users. Preferences can differ a lot with background, such as cultural influences and educational majors.

Unbiased questions

One challenge was keeping an unbiased perspective when writing questions. They had to encourage honest feedback, so participants could share their true opinions.

What I’d do next

Test the credit system

Test how people price their skills with credit points, since this was an open worry in the brief.

Test booking

Test the booking flow with real users, to see whether scheduling conflicts get in the way.

Build trust in skills

Look at how profiles and reviews help people trust the quality of each other’s skills.

How I’d do it now

Looking back, this is what I would take further today.

More research

Deeper research before designing, to understand the users and the existing platforms even better.

More screens

A fuller set of screens, covering the app from start to finish.

A refined flow

A flow where every step feels clear and easy.
